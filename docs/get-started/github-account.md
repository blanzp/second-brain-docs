# 0. Create a GitHub account

Unibrain keeps your notes in **GitHub**, a service that stores files along with their full history. Your vault lives in your own GitHub account, which is why your notes belong to you and not to Unibrain. You also use the account to sign in.

!!! success "Already have a GitHub account?"
    Skip this page and go straight to [creating your vault](vault.md).

## Sign up

A GitHub account is **free**, and it takes a few minutes.

1. Go to **[github.com/signup](https://github.com/signup)**.
2. Enter your **email address** and choose a **password**.
3. Choose a **username**. It becomes part of your vault's address, for example `github.com/your-username/unibrain-vault`, so pick something you're happy to see there. Your vault itself stays private.
4. Complete the short puzzle GitHub uses to check you're a person.
5. GitHub emails you a **code**. Enter it to confirm your address.

GitHub may then ask a few questions about how you plan to use it. You can skip them, and if you're offered a choice of plans, **Free** is all you need.

## Two things worth doing now

- **Use a password you don't use anywhere else.** This account holds your notes.
- **Turn on two-factor authentication**, so a stolen password isn't enough to get in. On GitHub, click your picture at the top right, then **Settings → Password and authentication**. GitHub will walk you through it with an authenticator app or text messages.

## What you won't need

GitHub is built for programmers, and its pages are full of terms like *commit*, *branch* and *pull request*. You can ignore all of it. After setup you'll rarely visit GitHub: you read and write your notes in the Unibrain app, and your AI assistants reach them through Unibrain.
